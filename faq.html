Frequently asked questions

Clear answers for international patients planning care in China.

Is this a hospital or a medical agency?

No. China Medical Index is an independent informational navigation service. We are not a hospital, clinic, insurer, or medical agency. We do not diagnose, treat, guarantee appointments, or provide medical advice. We organize free, public, authoritative information and can provide introductions on request.

How do you choose which hospitals to include?

A hospital must be officially Grade IIIA (三甲, China's top hospital tier) and meet at least one of: Fudan overall tier A++ or above, a national top-10 specialty ranking, or current JCI accreditation. It should also have an international department with English service. Every key fact cites its source.

Where does the data come from, and is it current?

Primary sources are the Fudan China Hospital Ranking, the JCI official directory, national and provincial health commissions, and hospital official international-department pages. Data was collected on 2026-11-19 and is scheduled for review every six months (next review 2027-05-19). Rankings and contacts can change — always confirm with the hospital.

Can a foreign patient get treatment at these hospitals?

Many Grade IIIA hospitals in major cities have international departments that serve foreign patients. English-language support varies by hospital, department and doctor, so confirm it directly before you travel. Visa, insurance and payment arrangements should also be made in advance with the hospital.

How much does treatment cost in China?

Costs vary widely by procedure and hospital. International departments and VIP services cost more than standard care. We publish indicative ranges with sources as a reference only — these are not quotes. Always obtain a written estimate from the hospital.

Is JCI accreditation verified?

JCI accreditation status can change and must be checked in the official Joint Commission International directory at the time of decision. Where we could not verify a hospital's accreditation from the official directory, the field is marked 'not verified' rather than presented as confirmed.
